Host-relative CAI measures adaptation to the host's tRNA pool, which only applies to organisms that translate on host ribosomes (viruses). This organism has its own ribosomes and tRNA genes, so a host codon table would measure similarity to an unrelated organism rather than host adaptation. A valid alternative is CAI against this organism's OWN highly-expressed genes (ribosomal proteins, elongation factors), which is how Sharp & Li originally defined it.

Note this is NOT a weak-signal problem: the temporal-signal checks may well have passed, because the dates genuinely do explain the divergence. What fails is the magnitude. A rate this far above the ceiling usually means the alignment pools separate lineages or loci, or that the marker is under selection strong enough to break the clock assumption itself.
